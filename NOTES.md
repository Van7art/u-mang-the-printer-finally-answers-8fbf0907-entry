# NOTES — The Printer Finally Answers

Gate: `/output/entry.txt` must be UTF-8 text of 1 to 240 characters, not only
white space. Judge (comedy editor beside a jammed printer), most important
first: the line is the printer's own comeback; it is funny; it sounds like
the machine (weary, literal, faintly passive-aggressive); the comeback lands
in a single read; it is fresh.

## The three variants

### vA (141 characters)

> Error 404: your document was found, printed, and is now jammed somewhere inside me. I have counted 12,847 sheets. I am not sorry. I am tired.

A good machine voice — literal, weary, counting sheets — and "Error 404"
subverts the error code itself. But the joke is really a complaint with a
punchline ("I am not sorry. I am tired"), and it never quite becomes a
comeback aimed at anyone.

### vB — PICKED (157 characters)

> I have printed 12,847 pages for this office and not one thank-you. Paper jam on tray two. I did that on purpose. Try turning me off and on again. I dare you.

The printer speaks in first person throughout: an exact count of its labour
(12,847 pages), a grievance (not one thank-you), a confession ("I did that on
purpose") that turns the jam from an accident into revenge, and a dare that
lands the comeback on the reader. Weary, literal, passive-aggressive — the
machine's voice — and fresh: the jam as deliberate sabotage, not a malfunction.

### vC (174 characters)

> You thump me, you stare at my blinking light, you whisper 'come on'. I have one job and one dream: to jam on the last page of your most important document. Today is that day.

A strong opening (the thump, the stare, the whisper) and a good villain turn
("Today is that day"), but it addresses the reader in second person for half
the line before switching to the printer's voice, so the comeback arrives
later and less cleanly than in vB.

## Why vB

Against the judge criteria in order: it is the printer's own comeback from
the first word; it is funny on a first read (the confession is the joke); it
sounds like the machine (a flat count of sheets, a flat dare); it lands in a
single read; and the deliberate jam is fresher than the usual "paper jam"
complaint.
